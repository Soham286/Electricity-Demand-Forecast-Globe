    What do these numbers mean?

    GW (gigawatt) measures how much electricity is being used at one moment. A bigger number means more demand. The expected range is where we think the real figure will land. % of the busiest hour compares this hour with the highest demand seen in the past year, so 100% would mean a new record.

    How the tests work

    Does the weather change how much electricity we use?

    How to read it: each dot is one past hour (or day). Left to right is temperature and bottom to top is electricity used. Dots rising towards the right mean hot weather raises demand (air conditioning). Dots rising towards the left mean cold weather raises it (heating). The orange ring is the hour you selected.

    How to read it: the blue line is real demand and the orange line is what the model would have predicted at the time. The closer the two lines sit, the better the forecast.

    What clues does the model use?

    How to read it: each bar shows how much the model relied on three kinds of clue: recent demand, the calendar (weekday, holidays) and the weather. A longer coloured piece means a more important clue.
